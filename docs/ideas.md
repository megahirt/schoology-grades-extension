## 4. Animations for what changed since you last looked

**Recorded:** 2026-11-15

When something is new since the person last saw it, show a short visual
cue as it scrolls into view: a change in a grade chart, a new assignment
due, a new grade.

- About half a second, slick, not in the way: stars, rockets, tada,
  fireworks, or similar.
- A different cue for each kind of change.
- The progress bar's rocket could fly along the bar when the week's
  count goes up.

Things to settle first:

- What counts as "seen": remember, per person, what was on screen last
  time (on the device only), and mark an item seen once it has scrolled
  into view.
- Two kinds of cue (decided by Chris):
  - Positive things get the fun animation: a good new grade, work turned
    in, the week's count going up, a class up since last quarter, a new
    record.
  - Everything else that is new just fades in, and that is its only
    animation: a new assignment due, a new Missing item, a lower grade
    (see the research notes on the features page: no red arrows for
    drops).
- Honor the "reduce motion" setting, and never repeat a cue for the
  same change.

Tone (from Chris): fun, but never corny or overbearing, and not
distracting. In practice:

- Small and local: the cue happens on the item itself, never across the
  whole screen.
- Short: about half a second, plays once, then the item looks normal.
- Quiet: the app's own colors, no sound, nothing that moves the layout
  or blocks a tap.
- Rare: one cue for the most important change in view, not one per item.
